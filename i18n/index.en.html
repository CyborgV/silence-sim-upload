How to play

In one sentence

Everyone decides whether to step out by watching what others do — and what they do, in turn, changes everyone else's decision.

Why nobody steps out

The regime can only arrest so many people each round. When only a few step out, every one of them gets caught — so nobody wants to move first. As the numbers grow, the same police force is spread thinner, each person's chance of arrest falls, and more people are willing to join. Below the “tipping point”, action is pulled back into silence; past it, a chain reaction begins.

What you can't see

  • Grievance: once punishment goes beyond the line people accept, every instance is remembered by those who watch. It doesn't show up as people on the streets, but it quietly lowers the tipping point.
  • Intelligence lies: the harsher the regime, the less people below dare to tell the truth — and the calmer the reported “public mood” looks.
  • Blackouts: what they suppress is not the memory, only its public airing. Once it comes out, it comes due twice over.
  • The enforcers are watching too: the crowd on the streets, how their comrades feel, whether orders go too far — all of it makes soldiers and police hesitate. Once they defect, fewer people can be arrested.

How to tell what you did wrong

  • Trend chart (left side; click it or press A to enlarge): people on the streets vs. the estimated tipping point, with every move by you and your opponent marked on the timeline. Enlarged, it also shows how grievance, troop morale and alert are moving; hover over it to see what happened in any round.
  • Advisor: ranks “the main obstacle now” (or, for the regime, “the main hidden risk now”) — too few people, too high a cost, not enough anger, or people unable to see each other — and points out which cards in your hand address it.
  • Undercurrent layer (top right of the city; L key): orange dots are people who are “wavering”, red dots are people “ready to move”. Dots growing denser mean an outbreak is getting closer; dots thinning out mean your strategy is failing. It is rumor, and can be wrong.
  • Report: two rounds after each card you play or policy you change, a purple “Report” appears in the news bar: how the numbers changed, how many were arrested, and whether the tipping point and the undercurrent went up or down.
  • Organizational strength (below your resources): when an action fails to cross the tipping point, most of those arrested are your core members. Keep sending small groups in and every later action will bring out fewer people; it recovers slowly while things are quiet. Careless card play has a price.
  • The ↑↓ next to the situation readouts show which way each value moved over the last two rounds: green is good for you, red is bad.

Play it like Plague Inc.

  • The two bars at the top: the left one is your progress (movement: how far from the tipping point; regime: how long you still have to hold out); the right one is your opponent (Regime alert / Opposition organization / Exposure risk).
  • Bubbles: whenever the situation shifts, bubbles pop up in the city. Click them to collect organizing power or political capital; they vanish after a while.
  • Build (hotkey B): permanent upgrades along three paths. Every movement build makes the regime more alert.
  • Cards: one-time use, with a cooldown. Cards that can bring people onto the streets show up as hatching on the progress bar — when the hatching passes the purple frame, it's time to strike.
  • Policies (regime): standing settings for enforcement, police, arrest targets, media and detention. The harder the line, the higher the cost.
  • Random events and historical events force you to choose. Every day brings its own headlines.
  • Time: pause (Space), advance round by round (→), or speed up. Number keys 1–9 play cards. When a level ends, the Debrief reveals the true values you couldn't see.
  • Time keeps moving (▶ by default). It stops automatically when you open a panel such as an event, Build or the menu, and resumes a moment after you close it. Space pauses; → advances one round.
  • Not sure where to start? Click “💡 Guide” at the top and the Duke of Shao will walk you through this level's screen and key points. It won't pop up unless you click it.
